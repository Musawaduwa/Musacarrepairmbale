How to Clean Battery Terminals (Even With Coca-Cola)

White, green or blue powder on the battery clamps blocks electricity. It causes slow starting, flickering lights and "no start" problems, and cleaning it is one of the cheapest repairs you can do.

Why it forms

Gas and moisture from the battery react with the metal clamps. Loose clamps, an old battery or a leaking battery make it worse.

What you need

  • Spanner (usually 10 mm or 12 mm)
  • Old toothbrush or wire brush
  • Baking soda and water, or a little Coca-Cola
  • Clean cloth, plain water, grease or Vaseline
  • Gloves and eye protection

Steps

  1. Switch off the engine and remove the key.
  2. Loosen and remove the negative (black, −) clamp first, then the positive (red, +).
  3. Pour a little Coca-Cola over the residue, or use a paste of baking soda and water. It will fizz.
  4. Scrub the clamps and battery posts with the brush until you see clean metal.
  5. Rinse with a small amount of clean water and dry completely.
  6. Reconnect the positive first, then the negative, and tighten firmly.
  7. Smear a thin layer of grease or Vaseline to slow new corrosion.
⚠️ Safety: Battery acid burns skin, eyes and clothes. Never let a spanner touch both terminals, and keep flames away from the battery. Cola works because it is mildly acidic, but it leaves sugar behind, so always rinse and dry. Baking soda is the better choice.

When cleaning isn't enough

If the battery is swollen, leaking, more than 3 to 4 years old, or the car still struggles to start, have it tested.
